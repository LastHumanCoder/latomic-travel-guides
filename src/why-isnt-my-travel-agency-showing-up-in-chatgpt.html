Why isn't my travel agency showing up in ChatGPT?

By Latomic13 min readFor travel agencies & tour operators

A traveler asks ChatGPT for the best agency for their trip, and it names a competitor instead of you. Annoying, but usually fixable. It is nearly always one of seven causes, you can check each in an afternoon, and most agencies are losing on just one or two of them.

Key insights

If ChatGPT skips your agency it is almost always one of seven things: a crawler that can't read your site, too few reviews on the platforms it trusts, no claimed profile, details that conflict across your listings, pages that don't say what trips you run, the wrong business category, or plain answer variation that isn't really about you at all. Work the list from the top. The early causes are plumbing, and if they're broken, nothing below them can help.

Seven reasons ChatGPT skips your agency

Trip planning moved to AI fast. Phocuswright found 56% of travelers used AI for at least one trip in the past year, with generative tools reaching 33% for research, and Skift put US travelers using AI to research a trip at around 41% (Skift, early 2026). ChatGPT reached roughly 900 million weekly users by early 2026 and carries around 80% of AI-chatbot referrals per Statcounter, so for a travel agency "showing up in AI" mostly means showing up in ChatGPT. Here is where agencies usually fall out, strongest cause first.

How travelers phrase it

The questions that decide your fate are specific, not generic. Travelers rarely type "travel agency." They type "small-group trekking operator in Nepal" or "family travel agent for Disney World," and each wants a precise page as its answer. If your site only offers a broad "Packages" page, the assistant has nothing clean to match and reaches for a competitor whose page says exactly what was asked. The fix is in how to get recommended by ChatGPT and making your travel website visible to AI.

Why the order matters

The seven are ranked for a reason. The first is plumbing: if ChatGPT cannot fetch your pages, nothing below it can save you. The middle causes are about proof, reviews and reputable mentions; the last about clarity and plain luck. Fix top to bottom, and you rarely reach the bottom.

Don't diagnose from a single prompt

One answer is not a verdict. Run each question several times, on different days and devices, before you conclude anything. A single miss can be noise rather than a signal, as the last section explains.

Check that AI crawlers can read you

OpenAI runs separate crawlers for separate jobs, and only its search crawler affects whether you can appear in answers. If your site quietly blocks it, nothing else matters, because ChatGPT has nothing to pull. This is the cause we find most often, and the one owners are most surprised by, because the block is usually accidental.

The search crawler vs the training crawler

This distinction trips up a lot of agencies. OpenAI documents its crawlers in its bots documentation, and the two behave differently. GPTBot is the training crawler; blocking it keeps your pages out of model training but does not remove you from search answers. OAI-SearchBot is the search crawler, the one that decides whether you can be cited when a traveler asks today. Plenty of owners blocked GPTBot on privacy advice and assumed they were safe. Allow the search crawler and you keep that door open.

robots.txt

Open yoursite.com/robots.txt in a browser. If you see a rule disallowing OAI-SearchBot, or a blanket Disallow: /, that is very likely your whole problem. Allow the search crawlers explicitly and keep your real destination and booking pages reachable. This is a two-minute check that can explain months of wondering why you never appear, and our guide to making your website visible to AI walks through a clean file.

Firewalls, CDNs and Cloudflare

The quieter culprit is your security layer. Many agency sites sit behind a CDN or a security plugin that turns away unfamiliar bots by default, and the defaults have hardened. Cloudflare now blocks AI crawlers by default on new domains, so a site set up in the last year may be refusing the exact crawler you want, with no one touching a setting. Open your bot-management rules, allow the AI search crawlers through, and confirm your pages return a normal page to a plain request rather than a challenge screen. Does ChatGPT use Bing, and where it gets local data

A fair question follows: if ChatGPT reads the web, where does it read from, and does fixing your own site even help? It pulls from several places at once, and knowing the mix tells you where effort pays off.

The search index versus the open web

ChatGPT's search feature reaches live web content through its own crawler and search infrastructure behind the scenes, then summarises what it finds. The practical point is not which index sits underneath, which shifts over time, but that your pages have to be fetchable and plainly written to be used at all. We cover the writing side in make your travel website visible to AI.

Where your local facts come from

For a specific business question, "who runs family tours of Rajasthan," the assistant leans heavily on third-party sources rather than only your homepage. Review platforms, travel directories and your Google presence carry real weight in whether you get named, as the testing below shows. It is also why claiming a profile matters for accuracy: our guides on claiming your agency and the ChatGPT Business Profile explain how to control the facts it repeats about you.

What this means for a travel agency

You win on two fronts: your own pages, which you control completely, and your off-site proof, which you influence through reviews and listings. Neglect either and the assistant hedges. OpenAI has also started building agent features that act on a traveler's behalf, and clean business information is what they rely on, as our ChatGPT business agent guide and the Business Hub guide explain.

You're thin on reviews travelers trust

Travel is a trust purchase, and the assistants behave as if they know it. In an independent test of 2,880 prompts across US metros, Steady Demand found ChatGPT's business answers leaned on review platforms far more than on any direct pull from Google: Yelp appeared in the vast majority, TripAdvisor in 28.9%, and Google itself in only 4.6%. If the agencies ChatGPT names have hundreds of recent reviews and you have a handful, that gap alone can explain the silence, however good your website is.

Why travel leans on TripAdvisor

The pattern makes sense once you picture the question. A traveler asking about a honeymoon or a trek is making a high-stakes choice, so the assistant reaches for the places where that reputation is recorded in volume, which for trips is TripAdvisor and, increasingly, Google reviews. The broader habit is moving the same way: BrightLocal found 45% of consumers used AI for local recommendations, up from 6% the year before.

How many reviews is enough

There is no magic count, because it is relative. Compare yourself to the agencies ChatGPT names for your kind of trip, not to a number in your head. If they sit on 300 recent reviews and you have 20 from two years ago, the gap is your answer. Recency matters as much as volume: a steady trickle of recent reviews counts for more than a large pile that stopped a year ago.

Earning reviews the right way

Ask every happy traveler for a review within a week of their return, with a direct link to the platform you most want to strengthen. Keep it steady rather than chasing a one-off burst, which looks unnatural and rarely holds. Never buy or fabricate reviews. The consistency work in ChatGPT vs Google Business Profile pairs with this.

ChatGPT has the wrong details

Sometimes you do appear, but with a wrong phone number, an old office address, or trips you stopped running two seasons ago. That can be worse than being absent, because a confident wrong answer sends a traveler to a dead number with your name on it. The cause is almost always conflicting details across your listings. When your facts disagree, the assistant can't tell which copy is right, and that uncertainty is enough to repeat the wrong one or hand the recommendation to a competitor whose details line up.

Fix the source, then the copies

Repair the detail at its origin first, which for most facts is your own site and your Google profile, then push the correction to every copy. Chasing a wrong phone number around the web one listing at a time, without fixing it at home, just lets the old value creep back. A claimed ChatGPT profile gives you one more authoritative place to state the truth, and the listing requirements guide covers what to supply.

Name and category drift

Two small forms of drift cause an outsized share of trouble. The first is your name: "Blue Horizon Travel" on your site and "Blue Horizon Holidays Pvt Ltd" on a directory look like two businesses to a machine, splitting your reputation in half. Pick one public wording and hold it everywhere. The second is category: being filed as a generic "travel agency" when travelers search for a "tour operator," or the reverse, can quietly keep you out of the answers you'd most want. Set the category travelers use, and keep it the same everywhere.

It recommends competitors instead

This is the version that stings, because you can see exactly who is winning. You can't tell ChatGPT to stop naming a rival, so treat the competitor as a worked answer and study why they're the one being quoted. The reasons are almost always visible on the surface.

Study why they win

Look at the named agencies with a cold eye. Count their reviews against yours. Read their destination pages: are they clearer, more specific, better matched to the exact question? Check how many reputable directories list them, and whether they state prices plainly while you keep yours behind an enquiry form. More often than not the winner isn't doing anything clever; they're doing the ordinary things more completely, which is the encouraging part, because those are the ones you can copy.

Close three small gaps, not one big one

The instinct is to look for the single reason a competitor beats you, but it's rarely one thing. It's usually three small ones stacked: a few dozen more recent reviews, one dedicated page for the trip type asked about, and a listing on two directories you're missing from. Fix the three together and you often move from unnamed to named. The method for building the pages that close the clarity gap is in how to get recommended by ChatGPT.

When it isn't you

Before you conclude you're invisible, rule out the possibility that nothing is wrong at all. Assistants are not consistent. The same question can name five different agencies across a single week, and signing in, switching city, or rephrasing slightly changes the answer again. A one-off empty result tells you almost nothing on its own.

Answers vary by wording, city and sign-in

Three things move the answer more than owners expect. Wording: "best travel agent for Italy" and "who should I book an Italy trip with" can return different names. Location: the assistant tailors local answers to the city it thinks you're in, so a test from your office and one from a traveler's home city differ. Sign-in state: a session with memory can lean on past context a fresh one won't. Record all three alongside each result, or it can't be compared next month.

Run each prompt ten times, then read the trend

The discipline is simple: run each prompt ten or more times over several days, note whether you were named, your position, which competitors appeared and what was cited, and look at the pattern rather than any single reply. If "family tours" keeps surfacing and you're never named across thirty runs, that's a real gap and your next page to write. Frequently asked questions

Why isn't my travel agency showing up in ChatGPT?

Usually one of seven causes: your site isn't crawlable, nothing reputable cites you, you have no claimed profile, your details conflict across listings, your pages don't describe what you do, you're thin on reviews, or you're in the wrong category. Work the list from the top, because the early causes block the later ones, and most agencies are losing on just one or two of them.

Does blocking GPTBot remove me from ChatGPT search?

No. GPTBot is the training crawler, not the search crawler. The one that decides whether you can appear in answers is the search crawler, OAI-SearchBot. Check that your robots.txt and firewall allow it; don't assume a GPTBot block covered both.

Does ChatGPT get its travel answers from my website or from reviews?

Both, but off-site proof carries a lot of weight for business questions. Independent testing found ChatGPT's answers leaned heavily on review platforms, with TripAdvisor in nearly a third of travel-relevant answers, so your reviews and listings matter as much as your own pages.

Will claiming my business make me show up more?

Claiming fixes your facts and gives you mention data, but it does not buy placement. Do it, then earn the signals that get you named. See how to claim your agency and the listing requirements.

How long do robots.txt changes take to matter?

There is no fixed timeline. Crawlers revisit over days to weeks. Make the change, keep your pages reachable, and re-check your visibility monthly rather than daily.

Should I optimise for ChatGPT or for Google's AI answers?

Start with ChatGPT, since it sends most AI referrals (Statcounter), but the work overlaps. Crawlable pages, plain-text facts, consistent details and real reviews help you across assistants, so you mostly do the job once. Our visibility guide covers the shared groundwork.

AI only sends a tiny share of my traffic, so why bother?

Direct AI referrals are still small, around a fraction of a percent of visits next to search (Statcounter), but that undersells the influence, because the assistant shapes the shortlist before any click. With over half of travelers now using AI somewhere in planning (Phocuswright), being absent from the answer costs bookings you never see.
