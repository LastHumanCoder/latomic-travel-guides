Why claiming matters for agencies

ChatGPT may already describe your agency without you knowing. It builds a picture from the open web, directories and reviews, only as current as the pages it read. For a travel business that has changed number, dropped a destination or rebranded, the entry in the Business Hub can be quietly wrong, and claiming is how you take the pen back. OpenAI set the Hub up so owners can correct their own listing, as it explains in its post introducing businesses in ChatGPT, and the stakes rose with use: BrightLocal measured local AI use jumping to 45% of US consumers, up from 6%.

ChatGPT may already hold the wrong details

The most common reason to claim is simple repair. An old number sends enquiries nowhere, a retired tour still listed invites complaints, and a wrong category points the wrong travelers at you while sending the right ones elsewhere. Until you verify the listing you can't correct any of it, and a wrong fact in an answer reaching part of 1.2 billion weekly users is worth fixing quickly. If the assistant doesn't mention you at all, the cause is usually upstream, which we cover in why your agency isn't showing up in ChatGPT.

Data you can't get anywhere else

Claiming also opens a window you have never had. Inside the Hub you can see how often ChatGPT surfaces your business and the questions it surfaces you for, part of business profiles in ChatGPT and a signal you can't get from Search Console or analytics. Those discovery topics, set against 56% of travelers now using AI for a trip, become your content plan. We explain how to use it in the Business Hub guide, and it feeds the pages you publish to make your website visible to AI.

The verified badge, and claiming first to protect your listing

A verified badge does two quiet jobs. It signals to a traveler that the entry is managed by the real business, which matters more in travel because a trip is a trust purchase bought months ahead. And claiming early protects the listing: leave it open and a competitor or reseller could try to claim or distort it, and unwinding that through a dispute is slower than claiming it yourself today. The same logic drove businesses to lock down their Google Business Profile.

A local agency with a street address and an existing listing has the smoothest path: there is already an entry to claim and a phone line to verify against. An online-only agency or OTA verifies through the website domain or a business email instead, which works just as well. Either way, the benefit arrives once the profile is filled in, so don't stop at the badge.

Claim it, step by step

The whole flow takes most agencies well under an hour, and the slow part is almost always waiting on verification. Work through the steps in order, with your business email, a phone you can answer, and a form of ID within reach before you start.

1

Open the Business Hub

Sign in with the account you want to manage the business from, then open the Business Hub from your account menu. This is the single place where claiming, verification, editing and insights all live, so bookmark it. If you manage several travel brands, decide now which account will own them, because moving a claim between accounts later is more effort than starting right. OpenAI's launch post describes the Hub as the owner-facing side of business profiles.

2

Find and select your agency

Search your agency's exact name. If a listing exists, select it and move to verification; if nothing comes up and you run an online business, look for the option to add a new profile. One trap to avoid: a near-match listing that isn't really you, such as "Blue Horizon Travel" when you're "Blue Horizon Holidays". Claiming the wrong one wastes the verification and muddies your facts, so match the name, address and website precisely to the business you control, as we stress in the ChatGPT business profile guide.

3

Verify the claim

Choose one verification method, covered in the next section, and follow the prompts. Pick the one you can prove fastest: a phone you can answer now, an inbox on your own domain, access to your DNS, or your Shopify admin. You only need to pass one. If a code or email doesn't arrive within a few minutes, check spam and your number format before retrying.

4

Pass the Persona identity check

After the business is verified, a one-time identity step run through Persona, a third-party provider, confirms you're a real person authorised to manage the business. Have a government ID ready, since this usually means photographing an ID and sometimes a quick selfie match, and it is fast once submitted. This step is about you, the manager; the previous one was about the business, and both must pass before editing opens.

5

Complete your profile

This step helps travelers, and most owners rush it. Add a clear description of what you do and who for, pick the right category, upload a logo and cover photo, and list your destinations, trip types and policies. Write it in plain text, not images, because the assistant reads words, a principle we labour in making your travel website visible to AI. A complete, honest profile is what turns a badge into a listing that earns enquiries.

The four verification methods

You only have to pass one of the four, which OpenAI lists in its business setup guide. Pick whichever is easiest to prove you control; they all lead to the same verified state.

Phone and email: the quick two

Phone verification sends a code by call or text to your listed business number; email sends one to an inbox on your own domain, such as hello@youragency.com rather than a free webmail address. Both are usually done in minutes, which makes them the default for most local agencies. The small print that trips people: the number must be the one on the listing, and a personal webmail address often won't pass, so use a mailbox that proves you own the domain.

Website domain: a DNS TXT record

Domain verification asks you to add a short TXT record to your website's DNS settings. It is the right method for online-only agencies and anyone without a verifiable phone on the listing, but it is also the slowest, because DNS changes take time to propagate. Add the record exactly as shown, then wait before you re-check rather than redoing it in a panic. If you don't manage your own DNS, it's a two-minute job for your host, and the method we most often use for OTA clients.

DNS takes the longest. A TXT record can take anywhere from minutes to a day to propagate. Add it once and give it time before re-checking; re-adding it only resets the clock.

Shopify: for travel stores on Shopify

If your travel store runs on Shopify, you can verify straight through your Shopify account, which skips DNS and email juggling entirely. So if your trips, gift cards or add-ons are sold through Shopify, this is the path of least resistance. Whichever method you choose, the Persona identity check still follows, so keep your ID handy no matter which of the four you start with.

What you can edit after

Once the business is verified and your identity check has passed, the profile opens for editing, where the ongoing value sits. Following OpenAI's profile fields, set your name and category, write your description, add your logo and cover photo, list the destinations and trip types you sell, and state your deposit or cancellation policies so the assistant can answer the questions travelers really ask.

Keep facts consistent with everywhere else

Whatever you enter here should read identically to your website, your Google listing and the directories you appear on. When your name, number or category disagree across sources, the assistant can't tell which is right, and that doubt alone can hand the recommendation to a competitor whose details line up. We go deep on this in ChatGPT vs Google Business Profile; it is the cheapest win most agencies have.

Multiple locations and brands

If you run several offices or trade under more than one brand, you can manage them from the same place. Keep each entry distinct, with its own catchment, destinations and contact details, so the assistant doesn't blur two brands into one. Agencies going further can also look at a ChatGPT business agent for travel agencies, which OpenAI launched alongside claiming per VentureBeat; it is a separate build layered on top of a claimed, completed profile, not a replacement for it.

Claiming won't boost your ranking

This is the part most guides skip, and it saves you from disappointment. Claiming makes your facts right and gives you data, and it is free to do. It is not a lever that makes ChatGPT recommend you more often. Placement still comes from your website, reviews, listings and the mentions you earn around the web, which is exactly the groundwork in how to get recommended by ChatGPT.

Why claiming and ranking are separate

Think of the Hub as the map pin and the website as the territory. Verifying the pin confirms where you are; it doesn't make the place worth visiting. OpenAI frames claiming, business agents and any future ads as distinct features in its setup notes, so don't expect a badge to do a recommendation's job.

Where recommendations come from

In travel, reviews carry unusual weight. In the 2,880-prompt test by Steady Demand, ChatGPT's business answers leaned heavily on review platforms, with Yelp most common and TripAdvisor second, while a direct pull from Google was rare. The real lever is readable pages, steady reviews and consistent listings.

Troubleshooting a claim

Most claims go through cleanly, but a few stall, and the fixes are usually dull. OpenAI's setup help covers the dispute path; here are the three snags agencies hit most.

SymptomLikely causeWhat to do
Verification stuck as pendingDNS still propagating, or code not receivedWait, check spam and number format, then re-check once
Verification failedDetails don't match the listingAlign name, number and domain exactly, then retry
Someone else claimed itListing taken by another accountUse the Hub's dispute path with proof of ownership

Pending or failed verification

A claim stuck as pending is almost always waiting on DNS or a code that landed in spam, so give it time and check the basics before retrying. A claim that fails usually means the detail you verified against doesn't match the listing: a number in a different format, an email on the wrong domain, or a name spelled differently. Make them identical first, then try again once rather than hammering retry.

Someone else claimed your agency

If the listing has already been claimed by another account, don't create a duplicate. Use the Hub's dispute path to prove ownership, with documents showing you control the business, domain and phone. It is slower than a clean claim, which is the whole argument for claiming early and defending your business profile first.

Still not appearing after you claim

Claiming the listing and appearing in answers are different things. If you've verified everything and still aren't surfacing when travelers ask, the cause is upstream in how readable and well-reviewed your site is, not in the claim. Frequently asked questions

Is claiming my travel agency in ChatGPT free?

Yes. Claiming and managing a Business Hub profile is free at launch, per OpenAI and as reported by VentureBeat. Any future paid features, such as ads or a business agent, would be separate from the claim itself.

Can I claim without a website?

Sometimes, by verifying with a phone or business email, but a website you own makes verification easier and gives ChatGPT far more to read. A proper site with destination pages is worth having regardless, as we explain in making your website visible to AI.

How long does verification take?

Phone and email are usually a few minutes. Domain (DNS) verification can take longer, sometimes most of a day. The Persona identity check is normally fast once you submit it.

Does claiming improve my ranking in ChatGPT?

No. It fixes your facts and gives you data, but recommendations still come from your website, reviews and listings. The method is in how to get recommended by ChatGPT.

Which verification method should I pick?

The one you can prove fastest. A local agency with a listed number should use phone; an online-only agency usually uses domain or business email; a Shopify store uses Shopify.

Someone else claimed my agency. What now?

Use the Hub's dispute path to prove you're the rightful owner, with documents showing you control the business, domain and phone. It is slower than a clean claim, which is exactly why claiming early is worth it.
