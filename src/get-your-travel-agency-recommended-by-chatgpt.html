How to get your travel agency recommended by ChatGPT

By Latomic10 min readFor travel agencies & tour operators

More travelers now ask ChatGPT who to book a trip with. This is a plain, no-jargon guide to getting your agency named in those answers, using the same levers that decide every other AI recommendation.

Short answer

ChatGPT recommends travel agencies it can read, verify and trust. Put clear answers on your site (destinations, trip types, prices, how to book), keep your name, address and phone identical everywhere it looks, earn reviews on the platforms it leans on, and keep your packages current. There is no paid shortcut to being recommended, and no single switch. It is the same groundwork that wins Google, pointed at a new reader.

Why ChatGPT recommendations matter for travel

Trip planning is one of the first things people brought to AI assistants. A traveler types "best agency for a two-week Japan trip with kids" or "who plans honeymoon safaris in Kenya," and ChatGPT replies with named suggestions and reasons. That answer now sits where a page of search results used to, and for many travelers it is the whole shortlist.

The good news for agencies: the work that earns a ChatGPT recommendation is the same work that earns a Google ranking and a confident human reader. You are not chasing a trick. You are making your agency easy to understand and easy to trust.

What claiming your profile does, and doesn't

OpenAI lets some businesses claim a profile so they can correct details and see how often they are mentioned. Claiming is worth doing, but be clear about what it is:

  • It does: let you fix your name, trip types, areas served and contact details, and show you mention data to work from.
  • It doesn't: guarantee you get recommended. Placement still comes from the signals below.

Treat claiming as tidying your entry in the assistant's address book. It makes sure that when you do get named, the facts are right.

1

Make your site answer traveler questions

Write the page a traveler would hope to find. If someone asks ChatGPT for a "family tour operator for Rajasthan," the agency it names usually has a page that answers exactly that, covering the itinerary, what is included, the price range, the best season and how to book.

  • One page per destination and trip type, not a single "packages" dump.
  • State prices or clear "from" ranges. Vague pricing makes the assistant hedge.
  • Spell out areas served, languages, and who the trip suits.
  • Make sure AI crawlers can read you. Check your robots.txt does not block them, and that a security plugin is not quietly turning them away.
2

Keep your facts identical everywhere

Your agency name, address, phone, hours and category should read the same on your site, Google, travel directories and booking platforms. When they disagree, the assistant cannot tell which is right, and that doubt is enough to hand the answer to a competitor whose details line up.

Pick the exact wording of your name and stick to it. "Blue Horizon Travel" and "Blue Horizon Holidays Pvt Ltd" look like two businesses to a machine.

3

Earn reviews and mentions

Travel runs on trust, so reviews carry real weight in what the assistants say. Ask every happy traveler for a review while the trip is fresh, and keep your profiles active. Getting named on respected travel directories and association pages adds a second kind of proof the assistant can lean on.

Where reviews land matters. Reviews are not all read equally by AI tools, and the thresholds are not public. Keep them steady and genuine across the big platforms rather than chasing one.

4

Keep trips and prices accurate

If you publish packages, keep the trips, dates and prices current. A wrong price does more damage than a missing one, because it costs trust the moment a traveler checks. Where you can, connect a feed or booking tool so what the assistant reads is what you actually sell.

5

Measure what the assistants say

You cannot improve what you do not watch. A business profile dashboard shows a rolling window of how often you are mentioned, what travelers ask around you, and where they click. Use the questions people actually ask as your list of pages to write next.

How to check if ChatGPT recommends your agency

Do this once a month. It takes fifteen minutes and tells you more than any guess.

  1. Ask ChatGPT the questions a real traveler would, in their words.
  2. Record whether you are named, your position, which competitors appear, what sources it cites, and any wrong facts.
  3. Note the date, the city and whether you were signed in, since answers shift.
  4. Run each question a few times; one answer is never the pattern.
  5. Track the trend across months, not days.

Frequently asked questions

Can I pay ChatGPT to recommend my agency?

No. There is no paid placement inside the recommendations. Any ads are separate from the answer itself. You earn the recommendation through your site, listings, reviews and accurate trips.

Does claiming a business profile help?

It helps accuracy and gives you mention data, but it does not decide placement. Claim it so your facts are right when you are named, then do the groundwork that earns the mention.

How does ChatGPT actually choose which agency to name?

OpenAI has not published the method. From what is observable, it leans on clear website content, consistent listings, reviews and reputable mentions. Those are the levers worth pulling.

Do reviews matter, and on which platform?

Reviews matter, especially in travel. Which platform weighs most is not published and appears to shift, so keep genuine reviews steady across the major ones rather than betting on a single site.

How long does it take to show up?

There is no fixed timeline. Fixing listings can change answers within weeks; building content and reviews is a steady few months. Measure monthly so you can see it move.

Is this the same as AEO or GEO?

Broadly, yes. Answer engine optimisation and generative engine optimisation both describe getting recommended by AI assistants. The work in this guide is what they amount to for a travel agency.
