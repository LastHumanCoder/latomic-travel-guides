Where Business Hub is in ChatGPT

Business Hub is OpenAI's dashboard for businesses inside ChatGPT. When OpenAI introduced businesses in ChatGPT, it did two things at once: it gave travelers a way to meet a named agency in the middle of a conversation, and it gave that agency a place to manage how it shows up. For a travel agency, the Hub is the control panel behind the mentions, and the place where your official details, trips and policies are supposed to live.

A new place travelers meet your agency

Think about where a traveler used to find you: a Google search, a directory, a friend's recommendation. Business Hub adds a new doorway, and it is a busy one. OpenAI built the feature to sit inside ChatGPT's roughly 1.2 billion weekly users, and separate reporting put ChatGPT at around 900 million weekly users by early 2026. The two figures come from different counts and dates, but the point holds either way: this is one of the largest rooms your agency can walk into, and the Hub is how you tidy the corner you occupy in it.

The habit behind it is already here. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 45% of US consumers had used AI to find a local business, a jump from 6% the year before, and Phocuswright reported that 56% of travelers used AI somewhere in a trip. A claimed, accurate Hub profile is how you make sure that when one of those travelers asks, the answer about you is right.

How you get into Business Hub

You reach the Hub by signing in to ChatGPT and opening the business dashboard from your account, then searching for your agency to begin a claim. OpenAI walks through this in its help article on setting up your business on ChatGPT. If your agency already appears in ChatGPT's answers, a version of your profile may exist before you ever claim it, assembled from public information, which is exactly why claiming matters: until you do, you cannot correct what is shown. The full click-by-click route is in our guide to how to claim your agency in ChatGPT.

Why it arrived now

Business Hub landed because travelers started treating the assistant as a shortlist-maker, not a search box. When someone asks ChatGPT to "find a small-group tour operator in Peru," the model needs trustworthy facts about who you are and what you sell. Left to guess, it leans on whatever it can read on the open web, and that can be out of date or thin. The Hub is OpenAI's answer to that gap, a first-party place for the business itself to confirm the details (OpenAI), which sits alongside, not instead of, the public signals covered in how to get recommended by ChatGPT.

Is Business Hub free?

Claiming your agency, managing the profile and viewing insights are free at launch. That is confirmed in the coverage of the rollout: VentureBeat reported that OpenAI lets you claim your business in ChatGPT for free, while noting that OpenAI has left the door open to charging for some capabilities later. So the honest position is: free today, with a watchful eye on what the company announces next.

What's free today

Everything a travel agency needs to be present and accurate costs nothing right now. You can claim the listing, write your description, set your destinations and trip types, add a logo and cover photo, and read the insights, all without a card on file. For most agencies, that is the whole job, and it is worth doing before any paid feature arrives, because an accurate profile is the thing that pays off regardless.

What might cost later

OpenAI has been clear that the product will evolve, and the launch coverage flags that more advanced capabilities, such as the agentic sales features, could carry a charge in future. Treat that as a reason to lock in the free groundwork now, not a reason to wait. If a paid tier appears, you will decide on it from a profile that is already claimed and already showing you data.

Ads are a separate thing

It helps to keep two ideas apart. A free listing is about accuracy: the facts the assistant repeats about you. Any future advertising or sponsored placement would be a different product with a different price, and being listed for free does not buy you a higher spot in an answer. We draw the same line in ChatGPT vs Google Business Profile, where the free-profile-versus-paid-visibility distinction is the same one travelers' agencies already know from Google.

Claim your agency

The first stage is finding your agency in the Hub and starting a claim. If a profile already exists from public data, claiming is how you take control of it; if none exists, claiming is how you create the official one. Match the exact legal and trading name you use elsewhere so the assistant does not treat "Blue Horizon Travel" and "Blue Horizon Holidays Ltd" as two businesses. Verify your identity

Before the full profile and insights open, OpenAI asks you to confirm you are entitled to manage the business, as described in its setup guide. This is an ordinary ownership check, the same idea as verifying a Google Business Profile. Have your business details to hand, and make sure the name and website you present line up with what travelers already see, because the pieces you need are the same ones in our business listing requirements.

3

Complete your profile

Once verified, the editable profile opens. This is where you fill in the description, destinations, trip types and policies that the assistant can draw on. A complete, specific profile gives ChatGPT clean facts to repeat; a half-finished one leaves it guessing. Treat this stage as the real work of the Hub, and the one that keeps paying off, which is why it feeds straight into making your travel website visible to AI.

What you can edit

The profile is the part you control most directly. It is worth filling out carefully, because every field is a fact the assistant may repeat to a traveler. Thin or vague entries make ChatGPT hedge, and a hedge is where you lose the booking.

Your core details

Start with the essentials: your business name and category, a clear description of what you do, your logo and cover photo, your website, and your contact and booking details. Keep every one of these identical to what appears on your own site, Google and the directories, because consistency is what lets the assistant trust the record. A single mismatched phone number or category can be enough to hand the recommendation to a competitor whose details agree with themselves, a point we labour in why your agency isn't showing up in ChatGPT.

Destinations and trip types

This is where a travel agency wins or loses the match. Instead of a single line that says "we do holidays," spell out the destinations you serve and the kinds of trips you run: family tours of Rajasthan, small-group Everest Base Camp treks, Kenya honeymoon safaris, budget city breaks in Europe. Travelers ask assistants specific questions, so a specific profile is a quotable one. The same specificity is the backbone of being recommended, which we set out in how to get recommended by ChatGPT.

Products and Shopify feeds for fixed packages

If you sell fixed packages rather than only bespoke trips, you may be able to connect a product feed so prices and availability stay current without you editing them by hand. Agencies that already run a shop, for example a Shopify store for set departures or add-ons, can point that feed at the profile so the assistant reads live details rather than a stale snapshot. A wrong price costs trust the instant a traveler checks, so a connected feed is worth more than a number you have to remember to update. How these feeds behave when an assistant starts to act for a traveler is covered in our guide to the ChatGPT business agent for travel agencies.

Reading the insights

Insights are the reason most agencies keep coming back to the Hub. They turn a vague feeling of "are we showing up?" into a few plain numbers. The trick is to read them for what they are, a rolling sample of ChatGPT activity, rather than a precise ledger of every conversation.

Mentions, clicks and discovery topics

Three figures carry most of the weight. Mentions tell you how often ChatGPT referenced your agency. Website clicks tell you how often someone moved from a ChatGPT answer to your site. Discovery topics tell you the themes travelers are asking about near you. Each is useful, and each has a clear limit on what it can tell you.

The rolling 28-day window

The single most important thing to understand about insights is the timeframe. OpenAI's setup documentation describes a rolling window, and that window is 28 days, not a live feed and not an all-time total. Numbers move as the window slides, so a quiet fortnight is not a verdict, and a busy one is not a trophy.

Insights are a rolling 28-day sample, not a live counter. A brand-new profile starts blank, and figures only settle once there is data in the window. Compare this month's window with last month's, pair it with your own site analytics, and treat the discovery topics as a to-do list rather than a scoreboard. (OpenAI Help)

Turning topics into pages

Discovery topics are the most actionable part of the Hub, because they tell you what to write next. If "family tours" or "solo female travel" keeps surfacing as a topic and you are not being named for it, that is your next page. This is the loop that quietly compounds: read the topics, write the page that answers them, watch whether mentions follow. It is the same monthly habit we build for travel clients, and it feeds straight back into the groundwork in making your website visible to AI. The insights show the demand; your site has to supply the answer.

Team and agency access

Business Hub is not meant to be a one-person login. A travel agency usually has an owner, a marketing hand and perhaps an outside partner, and the Hub lets you share management without sharing passwords.

Adding your team

Keeping access tidy also keeps your facts consistent, because fewer stray hands means fewer accidental edits that pull your name or details out of line with everywhere else. Multiple locations and brands

Plenty of travel businesses are not a single shop. You might run several offices, or a group that owns more than one brand, each with its own travelers and its own reputation. Business Hub is built to handle that from one place rather than forcing a separate login for every sign over the door.

One dashboard, many offices

If you operate in several cities, you can manage those locations together, which matters because travelers ask location-shaped questions: "a travel agent in Manchester," "tour operator near me in Austin." Each office needs its own accurate name, address and details so the assistant can match the right branch to the right traveler, and managing them in one dashboard is what keeps those details from drifting apart over time. Location consistency is the same signal that decides local answers in ChatGPT vs Google Business Profile.

Keeping brands distinct

A group with more than one brand, say a luxury-safari name and a budget-adventure name, should keep each profile distinct rather than blurring them into one. The assistant recommends a brand that reads as a coherent, specific business, so each one wants its own description, destinations and trip types. Managing them from the same place is convenient; letting their facts bleed into each other is not. If a brand's identity looks muddled, the assistant hedges, which is exactly the failure we unpick in why your agency isn't showing up.

What Business Hub cannot do

It is as useful to know the edges of the tool as its features. Business Hub is an accuracy-and-insight tool, not a sales channel, and treating it as a lever you can pull for rankings or bookings will only disappoint you. OpenAI frames it the same way in its own announcement.

No ranking boost

Claiming or completing your profile does not buy you a higher place in an answer. There is no slider that moves you up the shortlist, and a free listing is not a paid position. ChatGPT decides who to recommend from the wider web, so the way to climb is to be more readable, more consistent and better reviewed, not to tweak a setting. That is the whole argument of how to get recommended by ChatGPT.

No bookings, and no who-asked

The Hub will not take a payment or confirm a trip, and it will not tell you who asked about you. Mentions and clicks are counts, not contacts: you learn that interest happened, not whose it was, which fits how OpenAI describes the feature as a way to meet businesses rather than a booking system (OpenAI). The booking still happens on your site or in the conversation that a traveler starts with you, which is why your website and enquiry forms have to carry that last step.

Where recommendations really come from

The signals that decide your mentions sit outside the Hub, in your website text, your listings and your reviews. Travel is a trust purchase, and assistants lean on review platforms heavily: in an independent test of 2,880 prompts, Steady Demand found ChatGPT's business answers drew on review sites far more than a direct pull from Google, with TripAdvisor prominent for travel.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get into ChatGPT Business Hub?

Sign in to ChatGPT, open the business dashboard from your account, then search for and claim your agency. OpenAI walks through it in its setup guide, and the full route is in how to claim your agency.

Is Business Hub free?

Claiming, managing and viewing insights are free at launch. OpenAI has said some features may cost later, and ads would be separate (VentureBeat). For now, a travel agency pays nothing to claim and manage its profile.

Why are my insights empty?

Insights only appear after you have claimed and verified, and once there is data in the rolling 28-day window (OpenAI Help). A brand-new profile starts blank, so give it time and check back.

What counts as a mention?

A reference to your agency in a ChatGPT answer. It tells you interest happened, not who asked or whether they booked. Pair it with your own site analytics to see what the click led to.

Can I give my team or an agency access?

Does claiming my profile raise my ranking?

No. Business Hub keeps your facts accurate and shows you insights, but it does not buy a higher place in an answer. Recommendations come from your website, listings and reviews, which is the work in how to get recommended by ChatGPT.
