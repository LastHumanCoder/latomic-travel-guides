Who can claim a travel agency listing

A ChatGPT business listing is the verified profile ChatGPT draws on when a traveler asks it to recommend an agency, a tour operator or a trip. Claiming it means proving to OpenAI that you are the business, so you can correct the facts it shows and manage how you appear. OpenAI set the model out when it introduced businesses in ChatGPT: a listing exists, and verification decides who controls it. The requirements below are the gate before any profile work begins.

Do you need a website?

A website isn't strictly required, because phone and email verification exist for agencies that trade by referral or from a shopfront, per OpenAI's setup documentation. But for a travel agency a site on your own domain is close to essential. It enables domain verification, the sturdiest of the four methods, and gives ChatGPT real pages to read. Owner or authorised representative

You qualify if you own the agency or are authorised to act for it, which covers a marketing manager, an operations lead or an agreed agency partner. The identity check later tests that authority, so whoever completes verification should have a name and role that line up with the business records. Can you claim today? Eligibility by agency type

Eligibility isn't one rule for everyone. It turns on whether a listing for your agency already exists and on how your business is set up. The thread is simple: if a listing exists, you claim it; if it doesn't, you may be able to add one. Reading what ChatGPT already shows is half the battle, and our Business Hub guide walks through where to look.

Local agencies and travel shops

A high-street agency, a tour desk or any travel business with a physical address is the most straightforward case. ChatGPT likely holds a listing for you already, so you claim and verify it rather than create one. These agencies get the easiest path because they have a business phone and often a Google presence to lean on, and the surge in AI for local recommendations means a verified listing works the moment it goes live. If you run a Google Business Profile too, our ChatGPT vs Google Business Profile guide shows how the groundwork overlaps.

Online and home-based agencies

Agencies that trade entirely online, including home-based advisors and host-agency members, may not have an existing listing, because the signals that create one lean on a physical location. Here the path is often to add a new profile, which is where owning your domain decides things: domain verification gives an online agency a clean, self-controlled way to prove it exists without a shopfront, and Shopify is a second route for merchants. An online-only agency can still appear in ChatGPT's answers, which we unpack in why your agency isn't showing up.

Tour operators and DMCs

Tour operators, destination management companies and specialist trip builders sit between the two. Many have a strong website but a quieter local footprint, so domain verification tends to be the natural choice. Before claiming, get your public name consistent: the registered entity, the trading name travelers know, and the name on your site should agree, because the listing and the identity check both compare them. Once verified, a tour operator can turn the listing into a shortlist spot, and from there into recommendations by ChatGPT, the model OpenAI introduced for businesses.

Four ways in, one identity gate at the end.

Business phone and business email

Phone and email are the quick routes, and for a local agency often the easiest. Phone verification sends a code by call or text to the business number; email sends a link or code to an address on your business domain, so a proper you@youragency.com inbox is worth having. Both suit agencies without DNS access to hand, and OpenAI treats them as first-class options alongside the domain route in its setup documentation.

ChatGPT DNS TXT record verification

Domain verification is the sturdiest method and our recommendation for most online agencies and tour operators, because it ties the listing to an asset only you control. The Business Hub gives you a DNS TXT record, a short string of text, which you add to your domain's DNS settings at your registrar or host. ChatGPT then checks the record is present and confirms the domain is yours. The catch is timing, not difficulty: a DNS change has to propagate before the check can see it, anything from minutes to a few hours. Add the exact record once and wait rather than deleting and re-adding it, which only resets the clock. New to DNS? A partner usually handles this step, part of the claim we run end to end.

Persona identity verification

Whichever method proves the business, a Persona identity check proves the person, usually by having you present a government ID and sometimes a quick liveness check. For a travel agency, your ID and your business registration should tell a consistent story: the same legal name, the same entity. When the identity check fails, a mismatch between person and business records is the usual cause, not a fault in the document. This gate makes a claimed listing trustworthy and stops a competitor quietly claiming your agency. How long verification takes

There's no single number, because the four methods move at different speeds and the identity check is a separate step. The pattern below holds for most agencies, though OpenAI can adjust timings as the Business Hub, built for roughly 1.2 billion weekly users, scales.

Phone and email, in minutes

Phone and email verification are near-instant. A code arrives, you enter it, and the business side is done. If one doesn't arrive, check the number or inbox is the one published for the business and that nothing is filtering the message, then request it again. For a local agency this is usually the fastest path.

DNS propagation, in hours

Domain verification is the one to budget patience for. Once you add the DNS TXT record, propagation can take hours before ChatGPT's check sees it, and that delay is normal rather than a fault. The common mistake is to assume it failed and redo the record, which resets the wait. If it still hasn't verified after a day, confirm the record is in the right place and matches the string exactly.

The identity review

The Persona identity check is usually quick once submitted, often resolving in short order rather than days. Where it drags, it's typically because the submitted details don't line up with the business records. A clear ID and accurate business information first time keeps it short, and the Business Hub is where you track the status. An agency with its method ready and paperwork consistent can often be verified the same day.

Your business identity

Start with the name, the thing most likely to cause friction. Pick one wording and make sure your website, listing and registration all carry it, because "Blue Horizon Travel" and "Blue Horizon Holidays Pvt Ltd" read as two businesses to a verification system. Keep your government ID and business registration together, since the identity check wants them to agree.

Your website and domain access

If you'll verify by domain, confirm now that you can reach your DNS settings at your registrar or host, rather than finding mid-claim that an old developer holds the keys. A site on your own domain with a clear contact page serves double duty: it's the verification asset and what ChatGPT reads to decide whether to put you in an answer, the groundwork our guide to AI website visibility covers in full.

Your profile content

Verification gets you the keys; the profile is what travelers see. Have a short, honest description, the right category, a clean logo and your core policies ready so you can finish in one sitting. A profile that states your niche, say family tours of a region, gives ChatGPT a precise thing to recommend. The Business Hub is where you add and manage it, following OpenAI's setup steps.

Verification pending, failed or rejected

Most claims go through cleanly, but when one doesn't, the fix is usually small and specific. Resubmitting blindly only resets timers and muddies the trail. With ChatGPT now past 900 million weekly users, a stuck claim is worth fixing properly rather than abandoning. Read what state the claim is actually in, then act on it. The three you'll meet are pending, failed and rejected, each with its own remedy.

When a claim sits pending

Pending usually means something is still processing, most often a DNS record propagating or an identity review in progress, and the right response is patience. Give a domain claim several hours, and resist deleting the TXT record to try again, since that only restarts propagation. If it stays pending beyond a day with the record confirmed in place, that's the point to look closer.

When verification fails

A failed verification points at a concrete mismatch. For phone or email, the number or address you tried may not be the one published for the business, or a filter swallowed the code. For domain, the TXT record may be in the wrong zone or differ from the issued string by a stray character, so compare it letter for letter, then re-run the same method.

When a claim is rejected or disputed

Rejection, or finding that someone has already claimed your agency, needs evidence rather than retries. Re-check that your details match your listing and your ID matches your business records, since a rejected identity check almost always traces to that gap. If another party has claimed your agency, use the dispute path in the Hub and prove ownership with your registration and domain control, the model OpenAI set out when it introduced businesses in ChatGPT. This is why claiming early pays off, and why a managed step-by-step claim is the safer route when there's a dispute.
