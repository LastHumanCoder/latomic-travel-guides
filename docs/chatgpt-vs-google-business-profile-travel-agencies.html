ChatGPT vs Google Business Profile for travel agencies

By Latomic12 min readFor travel agencies & tour operators

A ChatGPT profile is new. Google Business Profile and TripAdvisor are where travelers and assistants already look. Here is how the three compare, where ChatGPT really gets its travel answers, which to claim first, and whether a small agency genuinely needs all of them.

Short answer

They are not either/or. Google Business Profile still drives the most discovery and feeds the wider web that assistants read. TripAdvisor and reviews do a lot of the talking in ChatGPT's travel answers. A ChatGPT profile lets you fix your facts and read mention data in the newest channel. Claim Google first, keep your reviews steady, then claim ChatGPT, and keep the same name, address and phone on all three. The overlap means you mostly do the work once.

Why this comparison matters now

For years, getting found meant two things for a travel agency: rank on Google, and keep a tidy Google Business Profile. What changed is that a second kind of reader now sits between the traveler and your website, and it does not click links the way people do. When someone asks ChatGPT "who runs small-group treks in Nepal," the assistant answers with a short, named shortlist, and for many travelers that shortlist is the whole decision. Your tenth-place ranking never gets seen.

Three profiles, three jobs

The confusion is fair, because all three look like "a listing for my business." They are not interchangeable. Your Google Business Profile is the record Google Search and Maps draw on. Your TripAdvisor listing is the travel-specific trust record that reviewers and assistants read heavily. A ChatGPT profile is a newer record that lets you confirm your facts and see how often you are mentioned. Treating them as one thing is how agencies pour effort into the wrong channel. The ground-up version of this is in our guide to making your travel website visible to AI.

Travelers moved first, the data followed

This is not a forecast. Phocuswright found 56% of travelers used AI for at least one trip, and BrightLocal found 45% of US consumers have used AI to find a local business, up from 6% a year earlier. ChatGPT reached roughly 900 million weekly users by early 2026. The audience is there. Whether you appear when they ask depends on which of these three records you tend and how well they agree. If you suspect you are already missing, our guide on why your agency isn't showing up in ChatGPT is the place to start.

How to read the table

The row that matters most is "what it feeds." Google Business Profile does more than show on Google; its data spreads across the open web that assistants search over, so a correct Google profile quietly helps you everywhere. The ChatGPT profile is narrower but more direct, feeding ChatGPT answers and little else. For a fuller walk through the ChatGPT side, see our ChatGPT Business Profile guide and the listing requirements.

Where the reach really is today

Do not let the novelty of the ChatGPT profile distort your effort. On raw referral traffic the gap is enormous: Statcounter has Google at roughly 99.91% and ChatGPT at roughly 0.09% of referrals. Yet ChatGPT's 900 million weekly users shape the shortlist before any click is counted, so that 0.09% can still decide who the traveler contacts. Keep Google first; do not ignore ChatGPT.

Where ChatGPT gets its travel answers

The most common wrong assumption is that ChatGPT reads your Google Business Profile and repeats it. Testing says otherwise. A study of 2,880 business prompts found ChatGPT leaned on review platforms rather than a direct Google pull, which changes where an agency should spend its time. BrightLocal's research into which sources ChatGPT uses points the same way, which is unsurprising when most travelers now use AI somewhere in planning.

Reviews do most of the talking

Travel is a trust purchase, and the data reflects it. In the Steady Demand test, Yelp appeared in 95.8% of business answers and TripAdvisor in 28.9%, the number that matters for an agency. A strong, recent flow of TripAdvisor reviews feeds the channel you most want to win, so ask every happy traveler while the trip is fresh. The same reviews help you get recommended by ChatGPT.

Google is rarely pulled directly, so what now

The same test found a direct Google pull in only 4.6% of answers. That does not make Google Business Profile unimportant, because it still drives discovery and feeds the open web. But energy spent on Google posts and photos, while neglecting reviews, leaves the ChatGPT channel underfed. In travel terms: keep Google tidy for discovery, treat TripAdvisor and reviews as the engine that drives ChatGPT, and let the ChatGPT profile sit on top. Getting named in the answer is its own craft, covered in getting recommended by ChatGPT.

The Foursquare question

Read around this topic and you will meet a confident claim: that Foursquare supplies 60 to 70% of ChatGPT's local business data. It gets repeated as if it were settled. It is worth slowing down, because planning your listings strategy around an unverified number wastes a quarter.

A note on the "Foursquare 60 to 70%" claim. We could not verify that figure against any primary source, and the 2,880-prompt test above found Foursquare cited in 0% of ChatGPT's business answers (Steady Demand). Treat the 60 to 70% claim as unconfirmed, not fact. The sources we can verify point to review platforms, Yelp and TripAdvisor above all, as what ChatGPT actually cites.

The claim, and why it spread

The claim likely spread because Foursquare runs a large places database that many apps license, so it is plausible on its face. Plausible is not measured. No primary source we found shows Foursquare as a majority of what ChatGPT cites, and the direct test (Steady Demand) points the other way. Time on a Foursquare entry is time not spent on TripAdvisor reviews, which the evidence does support.

What the test actually found

The measured picture is review-led. Yelp and TripAdvisor carry the business answers, OpenTable appears for dining, a direct Google pull is rare, and Foursquare did not show up at all. Act on what is verified and hold the rest lightly. Check any guide, including ours, for a primary source before you act on its claims (BrightLocal).

What is the same across all three

It is tempting to treat these as three separate projects. In practice they share a spine, and getting the spine right serves all three at once.

Your name, address, phone and category

The core facts should read identically on your website, Google, TripAdvisor and your ChatGPT profile. Name, address, phone and category matter most, and they are the ones most often left slightly out of step. "Blue Horizon Travel" in one place and "Blue Horizon Holidays Pvt Ltd" in another look like two businesses to a machine. Pick one wording and hold it. This single habit does more for your AI visibility than almost anything else.

Real, recent reviews

All three readers respond to reviews, and ChatGPT especially so for travel, with TripAdvisor in 28.9% of its business answers. A steady flow of genuine, recent reviews on TripAdvisor and Google feeds the trust signal every channel reads. Ask at the right moment, right after the trip, make it easy, and never buy or fake them. A faked review is a fact a later traveler will contradict in public.

Clear, readable facts on your own site

None of these profiles replaces a readable website. When an assistant wants to confirm a detail, it looks for plain page text it can quote: destinations, trip lengths, "from" prices, departure cities and contact details, written as selectable text rather than baked into images. Your own site is the one record you fully control, and the groundwork is in making your travel website visible to AI.

What is different

The spine is shared, but the three profiles are not identical. Three things genuinely diverge: what each controls, the data it shows back, and how you verify it. If you are still not appearing after the shared work, our guide on why your agency isn't showing up covers the usual causes.

What each profile controls

Google Business Profile controls how you appear on Search and Maps: hours, categories, photos, posts and the questions box. TripAdvisor controls your travel listing and the review surface around it. The ChatGPT profile controls the facts ChatGPT holds about you and gives you a hub to manage them, following what OpenAI documents in setting up your business on ChatGPT. None lets you dictate the answer, but each lets you make sure the underlying facts are right.

The data each one shows you

Google's insights are the richest today: calls, direction requests, how people found you. TripAdvisor shows reviews, ratings and views. The ChatGPT profile, through its Business Hub, shows how often you are mentioned, the clicks that drives, and the discovery topics travelers are asking about. Those topics are a content goldmine, because they tell you the exact questions to answer next. How you claim and verify

Verification routes differ. Google offers phone, postcard and other checks. TripAdvisor uses a business login. The ChatGPT profile offers phone, email, domain and Shopify verification, and rolled out in the US first, so availability depends on where you are. Start with Google, then TripAdvisor

Google goes first because it carries the most discovery by a wide margin, with roughly 99.91% of referrals, and its data feeds the open web assistants read. Fill every field, pick the right category, add real photos, keep hours current. TripAdvisor comes next, because ChatGPT's travel answers lean on it, appearing in 28.9% of business answers. Tidy the listing, match the facts to Google exactly, and ask for reviews after each trip. This is the work that most directly feeds the AI channel.

Then the ChatGPT profile

Claim the ChatGPT profile third. By now your facts and reviews are in order, so the profile mostly confirms what is already true and opens the Business Hub data to you. Because ChatGPT is about 80% of AI-chatbot referrals, it is the one AI listing worth claiming, and going last means you are not fixing facts twice. The fourth step, keeping everything identical, is the maintenance that holds it together.

ChatGPT and NAP consistency

NAP stands for name, address and phone, and consistency across these three records is the quiet lever that decides much of what the assistants say about you. It is unglamorous, it never feels urgent, and it is the thing most agencies get slightly wrong.

Pick one wording and hold it

Decide the single correct form of your name, address, phone and category, write it down, and make every listing match it to the character. Abbreviations, a suffix like "Pvt Ltd," an old number on one directory, a suite number on one profile and not another: each small difference is a seed of doubt. When your details agree, an assistant can trust them. When they disagree, doubt is enough to pass the recommendation to a competitor whose facts line up.

Audit every listing on a schedule

Consistency drifts as you update one place and forget another. Once a quarter, pull up your website, Google, TripAdvisor, your ChatGPT profile and any travel directories, and read the core facts side by side. Fix anything that has drifted. The stakes are higher in travel, a high-trust purchase, and given how many travelers now bring their plans to AI (Phocuswright, BrightLocal), a mismatch quietly removes you from shortlists. This ten-minute check is the upkeep we run for travel clients so it never slips.

Do you need all three?

For most agencies the honest answer is yes, because they do different jobs rather than duplicate one. The reasoning matters more than the verdict.

For most agencies, yes

Google is still where most travelers start and where the open web gets much of its data. TripAdvisor and reviews build the trust ChatGPT reads most heavily in travel. The ChatGPT profile keeps your facts straight in the fastest-growing channel, already the bulk of AI-chatbot referrals. Because the work overlaps, keeping your name, phone, address and category identical across all three is most of the battle, and a readable site, covered in making your website visible to AI, carries the rest.

When one might wait, and one job done once

There are sensible exceptions. If the ChatGPT profile has not reached your market, do the groundwork now and claim it when it arrives, since the readable site and strong reviews it depends on are the same work regardless. A brand-new agency with no reviews should pour its first effort into Google and genuine TripAdvisor reviews, then claim the ChatGPT profile once there is something real to reflect, remembering that 45% of consumers already use AI for local recommendations. The order bends to your situation; the groundwork does not. Get your facts right once, keep reviews steady, write a readable site, and you have fed all three in a single effort. Frequently asked questions

Is ChatGPT replacing Google Business Profile?

No. Google still drives most discovery and feeds the wider web, with roughly 99.91% of referrals to ChatGPT's 0.09% in Statcounter's measure (Statcounter). ChatGPT is a new, additional channel, not a replacement.

Does ChatGPT use Google Business Profile?

Only rarely in testing. A 2,880-prompt study found a direct Google pull in just 4.6% of business answers, with review platforms carrying far more (Steady Demand). Keep Google tidy for discovery, but do not expect ChatGPT to simply read it.

Does ChatGPT use TripAdvisor?

Yes, often. TripAdvisor appeared in 28.9% of ChatGPT's business answers in testing, making it the most important travel-specific signal you can feed (Steady Demand).

Is it true Foursquare supplies most of ChatGPT's local data?

That claim is unverified. We found no primary source for the "60 to 70%" figure, and the prompt test above found Foursquare at 0% (Steady Demand). Act on the review platforms the evidence supports, not on an unconfirmed number.

Which directories does ChatGPT use?

Testing points to review platforms like Yelp and TripAdvisor more than map or social listings (BrightLocal), though the mix shifts as platforms strike new data deals.

Which profile shows the most data about my agency?

Google's insights are the richest today. The ChatGPT Business Hub shows mentions, clicks and discovery topics you cannot get elsewhere, which our Business Hub guide explains how to use.

Can I claim a ChatGPT profile outside the US?

It is rolling out by market. Check your Business Hub for availability, and follow the steps in how to claim your agency once it reaches you.
