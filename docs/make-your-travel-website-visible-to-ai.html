7 ways to make your travel website visible to AI

By Latomic10 min readFor travel agencies & tour operators

ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI answers can only recommend what they can read. This is a plain checklist for making your agency's website easy for assistants to crawl, quote and trust.

Short answer

Let the AI search crawlers in, put your facts in plain page text (not locked in images or scripts), make your booking and contact forms work without a human, publish real FAQ answers, keep your details consistent, and check what the assistants say. Do those and you're visible to every major AI, not one.

1. Let the right AI crawlers in

Each assistant uses its own crawler, and a search crawler is what decides whether you can appear in answers. If your site blocks them, you're invisible before you start. Check your robots.txt and your firewall or CDN bot rules, and allow the search crawlers through.

2. Put the facts in plain page text

Assistants read text, not pictures. If your destinations, trip types, prices, departure points and contact details only live inside images, a slider or a script, they may be invisible. Write the important facts as real, selectable text on the page. A traveler asking "who runs 7-day Morocco tours from Marrakech" is matched to the agency whose page says exactly that in words.

3. Add schema, with realistic expectations

Structured data (schema) helps machines understand your pages, and it's worth adding for Google. Just don't expect it to be the thing that wins ChatGPT; clear page text matters more. Mark up your organisation, trips and FAQs, then focus your energy on the writing.

4. Make booking and contact forms agent-friendly

As assistants start to act for travelers, a form only a human can fight through is a dead end. Keep yours simple and readable.

Do thisQuick test
Real buttons and links, not image-onlyCan you tab to them with a keyboard?
Labelled fields (name, email, dates)Does each field have visible, plain text?
No pop-ups blocking the formCan you reach it without closing overlays?
Prices and dates as textAre they selectable, not baked into an image?

5. Publish real FAQ answers

Travelers ask assistants the same questions they'd ask you: visas, best season, what's included, how to pay, cancellation. Answer them plainly on your site, one clear question and answer at a time. Those answers are exactly the kind of text assistants quote.

6. Stay consistent and earn real mentions

Keep your name, phone, address and category identical everywhere, and earn genuine mentions on travel directories, review sites and press. Consistency tells the assistant which facts to trust; reputable mentions tell it you're real.

7. Check whether AI mentions you

Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity the questions your travelers would, note whether you're named and what they cite, and track it monthly. You can't improve what you never look at.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get my travel agency recommended by ChatGPT?

Be readable, consistent and well-reviewed. There's a full guide on getting recommended by ChatGPT, but it starts with the crawler and plain-text steps here.

How can I check whether ChatGPT mentions my agency?

Ask it the questions your travelers ask, note if you're named and what it cites, and repeat monthly. Run each prompt several times, since answers vary.

How do I get cited by Perplexity?

The same groundwork applies: allow PerplexityBot, publish clear text, and earn reputable mentions. Perplexity leans heavily on sources it can cite.

Does my website appear in Google's AI answers?

It can. Google's AI summaries draw on the open web, so being crawlable and clearly written helps there too. Note that AI summaries reduce link clicks, which is why being named in the summary matters.

Which AI assistants should a travel agency optimise for?

Start with ChatGPT, since it sends most AI referrals, then Google's AI answers and Perplexity. The work overlaps, so you're mostly doing it once.
