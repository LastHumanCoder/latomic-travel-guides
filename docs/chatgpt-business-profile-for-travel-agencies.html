Why a ChatGPT profile matters now

A few years ago, being found online meant ranking on Google and keeping a tidy listing. That still counts, but a second front has opened, and travelers are already on it. People bring whole trips to ChatGPT: "plan ten days in Japan with two kids," "who runs small-group treks in Nepal," "find me a travel agent for a budget honeymoon in Bali." The assistant answers with a short, named shortlist, and for many travelers that shortlist is the decision. A profile is how you make sure the agency in that shortlist is described correctly.

Travelers plan trips inside ChatGPT now

The scale is hard to argue with. ChatGPT passed around 900 million weekly users in early 2026, and OpenAI pitched its Business Hub at an audience it sizes near 1.2 billion weekly. Travel leans into this more than most categories: Phocuswright found 56% of travelers used AI for at least one trip in the past year, and Skift put the share of US travelers who used AI to research a trip in the prior six months at roughly 41% (Skift, early 2026). Being absent from the answer now costs real bookings, not hypothetical ones.

What a profile fixes, and what it can't

A profile is your verified set of facts inside ChatGPT. It fixes the problems you can actually control: the wrong phone number, an old business name, a vague category, a missing website. What it will not do is hand you a ranking. OpenAI is clear that the profile is about accuracy and connection, not placement, in its setup guidance. So think of claiming as the groundwork, not the finish line. If you want the fuller picture of what actually earns a mention, our guide on getting recommended by ChatGPT walks through it.

Where a profile sits in the wider AI picture

Local discovery is moving in the same direction as travel. BrightLocal found 45% of US consumers have used AI for local recommendations, up from 6% a year before. A claimed profile is one piece of showing up well in that shift, alongside a readable website and genuine reviews. If you are weighing it against the listing you already keep on Google, our ChatGPT vs Google Business Profile comparison lays the two side by side.

What a ChatGPT profile contains

Before you fill anything in, it helps to see the whole thing. A profile is a small, structured record: a handful of fields that together tell the assistant who you are, what you sell and how to reach you. Each one does a job in the answer a traveler reads.

The four field groups that make up a travel agency's ChatGPT profile.

Name, category and the verified badge

Your name and category are the first thing a traveler and the assistant read, so they have to match reality. Use your name as travelers actually know you, not an internal legal variant that appears nowhere else. The category tells ChatGPT what kind of business you are, "travel agency" or "tour operator," which decides the questions you surface for. Verification adds a badge that signals the facts are yours, not scraped. OpenAI covers how claiming and verification work in its announcement, and we cover the practical steps in how to claim your agency.

Description, website and policies

The description is where you say what you actually do: the destinations you specialise in, the kinds of trips you run, and who they suit. Keep it concrete, because a traveler asking about Kenya safaris wants to see "Kenya" and "safari," not "bespoke journeys." Your website field should point at your own domain with a working contact page, since the assistant treats your site as the deeper source behind the profile. Policies, booking terms, cancellation windows and deposits, matter in travel more than almost anywhere, because they are exactly the questions a traveler hesitates on before they commit.

Trips and product feeds

If you sell fixed-price packages, you can list trips or connect a product feed, so specific itineraries can show up with a price and a duration. This is optional, and plenty of agencies work on enquiry rather than fixed packages. If you do sell packages, a clean feed is one of the stronger things you can add, because it gives the assistant something precise to quote. The same facts should live as plain text on your site too, which is the point of making your website visible to AI.

Fill every field with traveler words

An empty or vague field is a missed chance to be matched to a question. Write the description, category and trip names in the words travelers actually type: destinations, trip types, seasons, group sizes. "Small-group Everest Base Camp treks" does more work than "unforgettable adventures," because it answers a real question cleanly. This is the same discipline that earns you Google rankings, so you are doing one job, not two, and it feeds straight into the listing requirements for travel agencies.

Keep your facts identical everywhere

Your name, address, phone, category and website should read the same on your profile, your website, Google and every directory. When they disagree, the assistant can't tell which is right, and that doubt is enough to pass the recommendation to a competitor whose details line up. "Blue Horizon Travel" and "Blue Horizon Holidays Pvt Ltd" look like two businesses to a machine, so pick one wording and hold it. This is one of the quiet reasons an agency doesn't show up in ChatGPT even after claiming.

A fully completed profile does two things a half-filled one can't. It gives the assistant accurate facts to quote, and it gives you a place to read how travelers are finding you and fix anything wrong. That reading side is worth more than most agencies expect, which is why we also explain what you can do in the Business Hub. A complete profile is the difference between being mentioned correctly and being mentioned with a three-year-old phone number.

How to update your business information in ChatGPT

A profile is not a set-and-forget thing. Prices move, seasons change, you add a destination or drop one. The ability to update your details is the whole point of claiming, so it is worth knowing where that happens and how often to touch it.

Where you edit your details

Once your profile is claimed and verified, you manage it through OpenAI's business setup, which is documented in its help article on setting up your business on ChatGPT. From there you can edit your name, category, description, website, policies and any trips you list. The flow is similar in spirit to editing a Google Business Profile: you change the field, save, and the corrected facts become the ones the assistant works from. If you would rather not log in and manage it yourself, this is the part we take off your plate.

What to change, and how often

Treat a few things as living fields. Keep prices and trip availability current, since a wrong price costs trust the moment a traveler checks your site. Refresh your description when your focus shifts, say you move into a new region or a new style of trip. Review policies whenever your booking or cancellation terms change. Everything else, the name, the category, the website, should stay stable once it is right, because stability is what keeps your facts consistent across ChatGPT and Google.
